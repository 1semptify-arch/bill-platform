PREAMBLE — Preamble & Definitions

Legislative findings and statutory scope across Minnesota residential tenancies.

P-1

Legislative findings modeled

+

Deposit disputes and serial nonpayment filings consume a third to a half of Minnesota's 23,282 to 25,847 annual eviction filings. Private 'deposit insurance' surety products pay the landlord and pursue the tenant through collection — creating new debt rather than protection. Custodial deposit protection with free independent adjudication has operated nationally in the United Kingdom since 2007, funded off pooled escrow float.

In plain language: Roughly a third to half of Minnesota eviction filings trace back to deposit conflicts or short-term income shocks. Commercial 'deposit replacement' bonds still bill the renter afterward. Putting deposits in neutral custody and funding dispute resolution from interest float is already proven at national scale.

D-1

Core definitions & scope modeled

+

'Residential landlord' attaches directly to the residential rental agreement rather than a municipal rental license, ensuring uniform statewide coverage across jurisdictions without local licensing. Defines 'Pool,' 'Fund,' 'Administrator,' 'Independent Adjudicator,' 'Covered Hardship,' 'Manufactured Claim,' and 'Clean Year.'

In plain language: Applies to any residential lease in Minnesota whether or not the city has a rental licensing ordinance, so rural and suburban rentals follow the exact same rules as metro units.

TITLE I — Deposit Protection

The self-funding custodial core. Amends Minn. Stat. § 504B.178. Modeled on UK Tenancy Deposit Protection (operating nationally since 2007).

I-1

Custodial escrow locked

+

Every residential security deposit is paid into bonded program escrow at lease signing and held there for the duration of the tenancy. The residential landlord never holds, commingles, itemizes, or unilaterally retains the deposit. Statutory one percent (1%) simple annual interest under Minn. Stat. § 504B.178, subd. 2 continues to accrue to the tenant.

In plain language: At move-in, your security deposit goes into a state-bonded neutral account instead of the landlord's bank account. It stays safe there until move-out and still earns your guaranteed 1% annual interest.

I-2

Independent claims adjudication locked

+ (1)

Deposit and unit-damage claims at termination of tenancy are decided by an independent adjudication body operating on an administrative-law model. Neither the residential landlord nor the administering insurance carrier decides claims.

In plain language: At move-out, if there is a disagreement over damage, a neutral referee — not the landlord and not the insurance administrator — reviews photos and records from both sides and decides who gets paid.

I-3

Tenant counter-claim lane locked

+

Tenants may initiate deposit return and statutory-remedy claims directly through the same independent adjudication body without filing fees; the administrator answers to claimants on equal footing.

In plain language: Renters do not have to pay court filing fees or take a day off work for conciliation court to get their deposit returned — they use the exact same online or mail claim lane.

I-4

No subrogation locked

+ (1)

A claim paid from the pool or escrow is final and is not recoverable from the tenant by the fund, the administering carrier, the landlord, or any assignee or collection agency.

In plain language: When the insurance pool pays a covered claim, the matter is over. Nobody can turn around and send collectors after the renter for what the pool paid.

I-5

Claims-free dividend (renter) locked

+

Tenancies completing a clean lease year with zero paid claims earn the renter an annual dividend declared from pool surplus above reserve targets, in addition to the statutory 1% deposit interest (mutual insurance model; declared from actual surplus, not a guaranteed fixed payout).

In plain language: Every year a lease runs cleanly with no claims, renters earn a bonus dividend when the pool has surplus on top of their guaranteed 1% interest.

I-6

Public claims registry locked

+

Aggregate per-owner and per-property-portfolio claims frequency and manufactured-claim findings are published in an open public registry, governed by Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)-modeled accuracy, notice, and dispute-correction rules. No individual tenant identities appear in the public registry.

In plain language: Renters and cities can look up a building owner's aggregate track record on deposit claims before signing a lease — with strict accuracy and correction rights for owners and zero tenant names published.

I-7

Manufactured-claim & false-referral penalties locked

+

A damage or hardship claim proven fabricated carries an experience-rating surcharge plus statutory penalty and referral. Symmetric penalties apply to any knowingly false fraud referral made against a tenant, with mandatory tenant notice, evidentiary threshold, and a defense window before any external referral transmits.

In plain language: Staging fake damage claims or making false fraud accusations carries matching financial penalties for both sides, and a renter always gets notice and a chance to respond before any fraud referral leaves the agency.

I-8

Self-funding of Title I via escrow float modeled

+

Net investment yield on pooled custodial deposits above the 1% statutory tenant interest (~3% net yield on Treasury-grade instruments, modeled at ~$23.4M/year statewide) funds escrow custody and independent adjudication, with adjudication carrying its own dedicated statutory budget line.

In plain language: Investing pooled deposits safely in U.S. Treasuries earns roughly 4%. After paying tenants their 1%, the remaining ~3% (~$23.4M/year statewide) pays for the escrow system and neutral referees without new taxes.

I-9

Regulation of private deposit-alternative products locked

+

Commercial security-deposit replacement products may be offered in Minnesota only if they include statutory no-subrogation, refundable principal or capped mutual cost, and mandatory reporting through the public claims registry.

In plain language: Private 'zero-deposit' companies can only operate in Minnesota if they follow the same rules: no chasing tenants for paid claims and full public reporting.

TITLE II — Rent Assurance

State-chartered guarantee pool administered on an unemployment-insurance precedent, beginning with an MHFA pilot on publicly subsidized stock.

II-1

Covered hardship rent assurance modeled

+

The Rent Assurance Fund pays covered monthly rent directly to the residential landlord during verified tenant income hardship arising from a defined statutory trigger list (involuntary job loss, severe medical crisis, or habitability displacement, verified under unemployment-insurance documentation standards) for a bounded coverage term (2 to 6 months, subject to actuarial schedule).

In plain language: If a renter loses their job or faces a documented medical emergency, the pool pays rent straight to the landlord for a set bridge period (2–6 months) while the household stabilizes — preventing eviction.

II-2

Forward-rent stability plan; zero backward debt locked

+

Covered rent paid by the Fund during a hardship window is not recoverable from the tenant. Any post-hardship payment plan applies solely to forward rent accruing after the coverage period ends and operates as a condition of continued ongoing coverage, never as back-debt collection.

In plain language: Rent paid by the pool during your covered emergency is forgiven, not turned into a mountain of back rent. Once coverage ends, any payment plan only covers future months going forward.

II-3

Three-source funding braid & mutual dividends modeled

+ (1)

Participation is mandatory statewide upon rollout and funded by three braided legs (~$15.33/unit/month base case at $1,300 rent, ~1.18% of rent): (a) Escrowed deposit net float (~$3.25/unit/mo); (b) Renter-side share (~$6/unit/mo) paid as a monthly advance from the Minnesota Renter's Credit (M1PR) reconciled on the annual return via CRP e-file rails (federal APTC / Form 8962 model; over-cap renters pay ~$6/mo directly, disclosed); and (c) Landlord matched share (~$6/unit/mo) priced on a community-rated base plus bounded experience band, earning a no-claim dividend in surplus years.

In plain language: Roughly $15.33/month per unit comes from three small shares: ~$3.25 from deposit interest float, ~$6 advanced from the existing MN Renter's Credit (or paid directly by high-income renters), and ~$6 matched by the landlord (who gets a rebate dividend in clean years).

II-4

Year-one reserve ramp & trust-fund backstop open drafting

+

Title II claim payouts phase in as custodial escrow crosses statutory reserve thresholds over the 12-month lease-migration ramp, or via a repayable state startup advance amortized against future premiums on the unemployment-insurance trust-fund precedent.

In plain language: Because deposits move into escrow gradually as leases renew over year one, reserve build is bridged either by a phased coverage start or a repayable state startup loan paid back by future pool premiums.

II-5

Tenant-side actuarial & anti-abuse controls locked

+

Solvency and fairness are enforced on the tenant side through UI-verified hardship triggers, per-lease coverage caps, claims-history pricing tiers at lease renewal, and mandatory cooperation with forward-rent stabilization plans.

In plain language: Protects the pool against gaming: hardship must be documented like unemployment insurance, each lease has a clear cap, repeat claims affect renewal tiering, and forward rent plans require cooperation.

II-6

MHFA subsidized-housing pilot authority modeled

+ (1)

Minnesota Housing (MHFA) is authorized to operate Title II first as a bounded pilot across Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) and agency-financed properties; audited pilot loss ratios and administrative data constitute the empirical fiscal note for statewide rollout.

In plain language: Minnesota Housing can test Rent Assurance first on affordable housing properties it already oversees, producing real Minnesota loss numbers before expanding statewide.

TITLE III — Renter's Belongings Coverage

Integrated contents line (fire, water, theft) riding the same pool; final cap and opt-in mechanics deferred to actuarial review.

III-1

Renter's personal belongings line (deferred parameters) open drafting

+

Personal-contents coverage for fire, water, and theft rides the same pool infrastructure as a third coverage line, coordinating habitability displacement with Title II as a single integrated claim. Mandatory vs. opt-in election, coverage cap, and deductible remain OPEN pending actuarial pricing.

In plain language: Adds basic protection for a renter's furniture, clothes, and essentials in a fire, flood, or theft — handled in one claim if a building emergency forces a move. Final price/opt-in waits for actuarial math.

TITLE IV — Governance, Home Agency & Review

Anti-capture board composition, MHFA home agency, administrative appeal standard, and mandatory 5-year sunset.

IV-1

Anti-capture governing board locked

+

The Pool Governing Board includes statutory voting seats reserved for residential tenants and independent consumer advocates alongside housing-provider, actuarial, and agency representatives.

In plain language: Renters and consumer advocates hold guaranteed voting seats on the board that oversees the pool, alongside property owners and insurance experts.

IV-2

Home agency & contracted carrier administration open drafting

+

The Fund is housed at Minnesota Housing (MHFA), claims adjudication sits in an independent administrative body, and day-to-day pool servicing is contracted to a licensed insurer (State Fund Mutual workers'-comp precedent).

In plain language: Minnesota Housing oversees the public fund, an experienced licensed insurer runs day-to-day claims operations, and a separate neutral body decides disputes.

IV-3

Administrative review & appeal standard open drafting

+

Adjudication determinations are subject to expedited administrative reconsideration followed by judicial review in Minnesota district court; the statutory standard of review (substantial-evidence record review vs. de novo) is flagged OPEN for legislative counsel.

In plain language: Either side can appeal a referee's decision — first through fast internal review, and then to district court if needed.

IV-4

Five-year sunset & mandatory Year-3 report locked

+

The Act carries a five-year statutory sunset from statewide effective date, paired with a mandatory Year-3 actuarial, eviction-diversion, and claims-registry audit delivered to the House and Senate housing committees.

In plain language: The law automatically expires after 5 years unless the Legislature votes to renew it after reviewing a full Year-3 audit of real costs, claims, and eviction reductions.

IV-5

Single-subject conforming clause locked

+

All M1PR advance-reconciliation and escrow-float provisions are enacted strictly as funding machinery dedicated to residential rental risk inside this Act, conforming to Minn. Const. art. IV, § 17.

In plain language: Keeps every piece of the bill focused on one constitutional subject — residential rental risk — so the credit-advance mechanics cannot be challenged as a separate tax bill.
