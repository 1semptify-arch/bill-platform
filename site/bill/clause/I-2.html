The draft / I-2

I-2 — Independent claims adjudication locked

Deposit and unit-damage claims at termination of tenancy are decided by an independent adjudication body operating on an administrative-law model. Neither the residential landlord nor the administering insurance carrier decides claims.

In plain language: At move-out, if there is a disagreement over damage, a neutral referee — not the landlord and not the insurance administrator — reviews photos and records from both sides and decides who gets paid.

  • Renter: Ends unilateral deduction letters; deductions require neutral review before money moves.
  • Housing provider: Valid documented damage is paid cleanly on a fixed administrative timetable without conciliation court.
  • Lawmaker & staff: Separates carrier administration from claim adjudication to prevent insurer capture.

The margin on I-2 (1)

M-0003 # · 2026-11-13T16:10:00Z · proposed edit · I-2 · Housing Policy Researcher

“decided by an independent adjudication body operating on an administrative-law model”

Proposed wording: Specify a 14-calendar-day decision clock from close of evidence and allow smartphone photo/video uploads without notarization.

In UK custodial schemes, clear timelines keep units turning over without holding up either party. Adding an explicit 14-day adjudication clock in I-2 would help both small landlords and moving renters.

Statutory Review Lane: Strong operational point — logged for Title I statutory text integration alongside Minn. Stat. § 504B.178's existing 21-day baseline.
