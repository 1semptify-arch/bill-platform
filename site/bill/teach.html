Teach the bill

A guided path through the draft for classrooms, tenant meetings, and kitchen-table briefings — 14 stops, plain language first, each with a discussion prompt. Pair with the TV briefing deck or have students follow along in plain-language view.

Stop 01 · P-1

Why this bill exists

Plain language: Roughly a third to half of Minnesota eviction filings trace back to deposit conflicts or short-term income shocks. Commercial 'deposit replacement' bonds still bill the renter afterward. Putting deposits in neutral custody and funding dispute resolution from interest float is already proven at national scale.

Talk about: Open question: who holds your deposit today — and who decides when there's a fight?

Stop 02 · D-1

The words of the bill

Plain language: Applies to any residential lease in Minnesota whether or not the city has a rental licensing ordinance, so rural and suburban rentals follow the exact same rules as metro units.

Talk about: Why does coverage attach to the lease, not a city license?

Stop 03 · I-1

Where the deposit goes

Plain language: At move-in, your security deposit goes into a state-bonded neutral account instead of the landlord's bank account. It stays safe there until move-out and still earns your guaranteed 1% annual interest.

Talk about: What actually changes at lease signing?

Stop 04 · I-2

Who decides disputes

Plain language: At move-out, if there is a disagreement over damage, a neutral referee — not the landlord and not the insurance administrator — reviews photos and records from both sides and decides who gets paid.

Talk about: Why can't the landlord — or the insurer — decide claims?

Stop 05 · I-3

When you're the one owed

Plain language: Renters do not have to pay court filing fees or take a day off work for conciliation court to get their deposit returned — they use the exact same online or mail claim lane.

Talk about: What does a free counter-claim lane change for renters?

Stop 06 · I-4

A paid claim is final

Plain language: When the insurance pool pays a covered claim, the matter is over. Nobody can turn around and send collectors after the renter for what the pool paid.

Talk about: Why does no-subrogation matter more than the payout?

Stop 07 · I-5

Rewards for clean years

Plain language: Every year a lease runs cleanly with no claims, renters earn a bonus dividend when the pool has surplus on top of their guaranteed 1% interest.

Talk about: Who earns the dividend, and where does the surplus come from?

Stop 08 · I-6

Checking the record

Plain language: Renters and cities can look up a building owner's aggregate track record on deposit claims before signing a lease — with strict accuracy and correction rights for owners and zero tenant names published.

Talk about: What could you look up about a building before you sign?

Stop 09 · II-1

Emergency rent coverage

Plain language: If a renter loses their job or faces a documented medical emergency, the pool pays rent straight to the landlord for a set bridge period (2–6 months) while the household stabilizes — preventing eviction.

Talk about: Which hardships trigger the fund — and which don't?

Stop 10 · II-2

No debt cliff

Plain language: Rent paid by the pool during your covered emergency is forgiven, not turned into a mountain of back rent. Once coverage ends, any payment plan only covers future months going forward.

Talk about: What happens to a family the month after coverage ends?

Stop 11 · II-3

Who pays what

Plain language: Roughly $15.33/month per unit comes from three small shares: ~$3.25 from deposit interest float, ~$6 advanced from the existing MN Renter's Credit (or paid directly by high-income renters), and ~$6 matched by the landlord (who gets a rebate dividend in clean years).

Talk about: Trace the three funding legs — then test your own rent on the calculator.

Stop 12 · III-1

Your belongings

Plain language: Adds basic protection for a renter's furniture, clothes, and essentials in a fire, flood, or theft — handled in one claim if a building emergency forces a move. Final price/opt-in waits for actuarial math.

Talk about: What does belongings coverage add beyond the deposit?

Stop 13 · IV-1

Who runs it

Plain language: Renters and consumer advocates hold guaranteed voting seats on the board that oversees the pool, alongside property owners and insurance experts.

Talk about: Why a home agency instead of bolting it onto an existing one?

Stop 14 · IV-5

Staying honest

Plain language: Keeps every piece of the bill focused on one constitutional subject — residential rental risk — so the credit-advance mechanics cannot be challenged as a separate tax bill.

Talk about: How does the public registry keep the system accountable?
