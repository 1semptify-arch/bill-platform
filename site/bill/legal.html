Legal

The fine print, kept readable — privacy, terms, and license for this platform. The bill is a working draft: nothing here is filed law, and nothing here is legal advice.

Privacy

What we store. Only what you choose to submit: the text of marks and requests, and — if you opt in — a name, contact, or email. Signing the signature book stores what you typed; your name, org/town, and line publish only after a steward reads them in. Email and contact fields are never rendered publicly.

Receipts. Each submission returns a private code for status checks. It reveals status only — never your contact or full text.

Cookies. Two functional cookies, no tracking: bp_mode (your screen-mode choice) and bp_steward (steward session). No analytics, no ads, no third-party trackers — there is nothing to opt out of.

Rate limiting. Submission counts are kept per IP in memory only; nothing is written to the database about your IP.

Where it lives. Records sit in a database on the hosting service and replicate to project cloud storage for backup. Stewards can export the full record for safekeeping. To ask for a correction or removal, leave a steward note through Requests.

Terms

  • This platform presents a draft bill for public reading, comment, and campaign coordination. It is not enacted law and confers no rights or obligations.
  • Submissions are reviewed by people before anything publishes. The stewards may hold, decline, or retract material — the record of that decision stays visible.
  • Don't submit other people's personal information. Yours is your choice; theirs is not.
  • No abusive, deceptive, or commercial content. This space carries no advertising and promotes no product or service.
  • Nothing here is legal advice. If you have a live housing problem, the help lines are the right door.

License

Content. The bill text, plain-language explanations, and page content are dedicated to the public domain (CC0 1.0) — take them, use them, carry them. A bill belongs to the public.

Code. The platform source is released under the MIT License.

Marks and signatures. By submitting, you agree your words (and for signatures, the name you consent to publish) may be read into the public record. Retraction stays on the record rather than erasing it — that's what makes the ledger honest.
