State of Minnesota · Legislative Draft Packet

Minnesota Rent Assurance & Deposit Protection Act

Turnbuchalla Model — Working Statutory Draft — amends Minn. Stat. § 504B.178
Move every residential security deposit into bonded custodial escrow, replace unilateral deposit deductions with independent adjudication, and back verified tenant hardship with a three-source rent assurance pool — with zero subrogation debt.
Stage: Working Draft — Pre-Introduction  ·  Updated: 2026-11-14
Written in public at semptify.org/bill · printed from the canonical working draft

The Bill at a Glance

Title I · Deposit Escrow — Landlords never hold deposits; neutral adjudicators decide claims.Deposits sit in bonded custodial escrow for the tenancy. Tenants earn statutory 1% interest plus clean-year surplus dividends; pooled net float (~3%, ~$23.4M/yr at scale) funds administration and independent adjudication. (clause I-1)
Title II · Rent Assurance — Verified hardship rent is paid from a three-source pool — never clawed back.During verified job loss or medical crisis, the pool pays covered rent directly to the housing provider. Funded by ~3% deposit float (~$3.25/mo), monthly M1PR tax-credit advance (~$6/mo), and matched landlord share (~$6/mo). (clause II-3)
Fairness & Anti-Capture — Symmetric fraud penalties, public claims registry, and a 5-year sunset.Paid claims end the matter (no subrogation). Fabricated landlord claims and false fraud referrals carry symmetric penalties; tenant abuse is bounded by verified triggers, per-lease caps, and renewal pricing. (clause I-4)

PREAMBLE — Preamble & Definitions

Legislative findings and statutory scope across Minnesota residential tenancies.

P-1Legislative findingsmodeled

Statutory draft
Deposit disputes and serial nonpayment filings consume a third to a half of Minnesota's 23,282 to 25,847 annual eviction filings. Private 'deposit insurance' surety products pay the landlord and pursue the tenant through collection — creating new debt rather than protection. Custodial deposit protection with free independent adjudication has operated nationally in the United Kingdom since 2007, funded off pooled escrow float.
Plain language
Roughly a third to half of Minnesota eviction filings trace back to deposit conflicts or short-term income shocks. Commercial 'deposit replacement' bonds still bill the renter afterward. Putting deposits in neutral custody and funding dispute resolution from interest float is already proven at national scale.
Renter: Explains why fee-based 'deposit alternatives' leave renters owing money at move-out.
Provider: Recognizes the $5,600–$7,400 provider loss per eviction that fast assurance prevents.
Lawmaker: Grounds single-subject jurisdiction ('residential rental risk') in MN Judicial Branch filing data.
Sources: C-02, C-04, C-05

D-1Core definitions & scopemodeled

Statutory draft
'Residential landlord' attaches directly to the residential rental agreement rather than a municipal rental license, ensuring uniform statewide coverage across jurisdictions without local licensing. Defines 'Pool,' 'Fund,' 'Administrator,' 'Independent Adjudicator,' 'Covered Hardship,' 'Manufactured Claim,' and 'Clean Year.'
Plain language
Applies to any residential lease in Minnesota whether or not the city has a rental licensing ordinance, so rural and suburban rentals follow the exact same rules as metro units.
Renter: Covers your lease anywhere in Minnesota, regardless of city licensing rules.
Provider: One uniform statewide definition — no patchwork municipal compliance.
Lawmaker: Avoids Minnesota's municipal-licensing gap by attaching to the lease relationship under Ch. 504B.
Sources: C-01

TITLE I — Deposit Protection

The self-funding custodial core. Amends Minn. Stat. § 504B.178. Modeled on UK Tenancy Deposit Protection (operating nationally since 2007).

I-1Custodial escrowlocked

Statutory draft
Every residential security deposit is paid into bonded program escrow at lease signing and held there for the duration of the tenancy. The residential landlord never holds, commingles, itemizes, or unilaterally retains the deposit. Statutory one percent (1%) simple annual interest under Minn. Stat. § 504B.178, subd. 2 continues to accrue to the tenant.
Plain language
At move-in, your security deposit goes into a state-bonded neutral account instead of the landlord's bank account. It stays safe there until move-out and still earns your guaranteed 1% annual interest.
Renter: Your deposit cannot be spent, lost in a property sale, or held hostage at move-out.
Provider: Eliminates separate trust-account bookkeeping and 21-day statutory penalty traps.
Lawmaker: Directly amends Minn. Stat. § 504B.178 custodial mechanics; preserves the 1% tenant floor.
Sources: C-01, C-10

I-2Independent claims adjudicationlocked

Statutory draft
Deposit and unit-damage claims at termination of tenancy are decided by an independent adjudication body operating on an administrative-law model. Neither the residential landlord nor the administering insurance carrier decides claims.
Plain language
At move-out, if there is a disagreement over damage, a neutral referee — not the landlord and not the insurance administrator — reviews photos and records from both sides and decides who gets paid.
Renter: Ends unilateral deduction letters; deductions require neutral review before money moves.
Provider: Valid documented damage is paid cleanly on a fixed administrative timetable without conciliation court.
Lawmaker: Separates carrier administration from claim adjudication to prevent insurer capture.
Sources: C-10

I-3Tenant counter-claim lanelocked

Statutory draft
Tenants may initiate deposit return and statutory-remedy claims directly through the same independent adjudication body without filing fees; the administrator answers to claimants on equal footing.
Plain language
Renters do not have to pay court filing fees or take a day off work for conciliation court to get their deposit returned — they use the exact same online or mail claim lane.
Renter: Zero filing fee to request your deposit return if a landlord fails to sign off at move-out.
Provider: Resolves move-out disputes in days without courtroom legal fees.
Lawmaker: Diverts high-volume § 504B.178 deposit disputes out of district and conciliation court dockets.
Sources: C-02

I-4No subrogationlocked

Statutory draft
A claim paid from the pool or escrow is final and is not recoverable from the tenant by the fund, the administering carrier, the landlord, or any assignee or collection agency.
Plain language
When the insurance pool pays a covered claim, the matter is over. Nobody can turn around and send collectors after the renter for what the pool paid.
Renter: Locked non-negotiable: true insurance protection, never a disguised surety loan.
Provider: You get paid promptly without chasing former tenants through collection agencies.
Lawmaker: The core statutory distinction between pooled insurance and commercial surety-bond products.
Sources: C-04, C-06

I-5Claims-free dividend (renter)locked

Statutory draft
Tenancies completing a clean lease year with zero paid claims earn the renter an annual dividend declared from pool surplus above reserve targets, in addition to the statutory 1% deposit interest (mutual insurance model; declared from actual surplus, not a guaranteed fixed payout).
Plain language
Every year a lease runs cleanly with no claims, renters earn a bonus dividend when the pool has surplus on top of their guaranteed 1% interest.
Renter: Rewards taking care of your home with an annual cash or credit dividend in surplus years.
Provider: Aligns tenant financial incentives directly with unit care and lease compliance.
Lawmaker: Mutual-pool surplus mechanics (SFM precedent) avoid state general-fund liability.
Sources: C-01, C-09

I-6Public claims registrylocked

Statutory draft
Aggregate per-owner and per-property-portfolio claims frequency and manufactured-claim findings are published in an open public registry, governed by Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)-modeled accuracy, notice, and dispute-correction rules. No individual tenant identities appear in the public registry.
Plain language
Renters and cities can look up a building owner's aggregate track record on deposit claims before signing a lease — with strict accuracy and correction rights for owners and zero tenant names published.
Renter: See whether a building routinely files inflated move-out claims before you rent there.
Provider: Clean operators can prove their fair track record publicly; FCRA dispute rules protect against errors.
Lawmaker: Replaces opaque anecdotes with auditable portfolio-level data while keeping zero tenant PII.
Sources: C-03

I-7Manufactured-claim & false-referral penaltieslocked

Statutory draft
A damage or hardship claim proven fabricated carries an experience-rating surcharge plus statutory penalty and referral. Symmetric penalties apply to any knowingly false fraud referral made against a tenant, with mandatory tenant notice, evidentiary threshold, and a defense window before any external referral transmits.
Plain language
Staging fake damage claims or making false fraud accusations carries matching financial penalties for both sides, and a renter always gets notice and a chance to respond before any fraud referral leaves the agency.
Renter: Blocks retaliatory 'fraud' threats by requiring real proof, prior notice, and symmetric penalties.
Provider: Deters fraudulent tenant hardship filings alongside bad-actor landlord claims.
Lawmaker: Symmetric deterrence satisfies due-process and bipartisan fairness scrutiny.
Sources: C-08

I-8Self-funding of Title I via escrow floatmodeled

Statutory draft
Net investment yield on pooled custodial deposits above the 1% statutory tenant interest (~3% net yield on Treasury-grade instruments, modeled at ~$23.4M/year statewide) funds escrow custody and independent adjudication, with adjudication carrying its own dedicated statutory budget line.
Plain language
Investing pooled deposits safely in U.S. Treasuries earns roughly 4%. After paying tenants their 1%, the remaining ~3% (~$23.4M/year statewide) pays for the escrow system and neutral referees without new taxes.
Renter: No fee is deducted from your deposit principal to run the escrow or dispute system.
Provider: No per-unit escrow administrative fee.
Lawmaker: Dedicated adjudication budget line prevents administrative starving of neutral review.
Sources: C-01, C-10

I-9Regulation of private deposit-alternative productslocked

Statutory draft
Commercial security-deposit replacement products may be offered in Minnesota only if they include statutory no-subrogation, refundable principal or capped mutual cost, and mandatory reporting through the public claims registry.
Plain language
Private 'zero-deposit' companies can only operate in Minnesota if they follow the same rules: no chasing tenants for paid claims and full public reporting.
Renter: Closes the loophole where monthly 'deposit fee' apps charge non-refundable fees and still sue you.
Provider: Levels the playing field so compliant coverage isn't undercut by predatory surety bonds.
Lawmaker: Prevents regulatory arbitrage around Minn. Stat. § 504B.178.
Sources: C-06

TITLE II — Rent Assurance

State-chartered guarantee pool administered on an unemployment-insurance precedent, beginning with an MHFA pilot on publicly subsidized stock.

II-1Covered hardship rent assurancemodeled

Statutory draft
The Rent Assurance Fund pays covered monthly rent directly to the residential landlord during verified tenant income hardship arising from a defined statutory trigger list (involuntary job loss, severe medical crisis, or habitability displacement, verified under unemployment-insurance documentation standards) for a bounded coverage term (2 to 6 months, subject to actuarial schedule).
Plain language
If a renter loses their job or faces a documented medical emergency, the pool pays rent straight to the landlord for a set bridge period (2–6 months) while the household stabilizes — preventing eviction.
Renter: Keeps your family housed and keeps an eviction filing off your court record during a real crisis.
Provider: Rent arrives on time during tenant emergencies instead of months of unpaid rent and court costs.
Lawmaker: Uses proven UI verification triggers; coverage term (2–6 mo) flagged for actuarial calibration.
Sources: C-02, C-05, C-07

II-2Forward-rent stability plan; zero backward debtlocked

Statutory draft
Covered rent paid by the Fund during a hardship window is not recoverable from the tenant. Any post-hardship payment plan applies solely to forward rent accruing after the coverage period ends and operates as a condition of continued ongoing coverage, never as back-debt collection.
Plain language
Rent paid by the pool during your covered emergency is forgiven, not turned into a mountain of back rent. Once coverage ends, any payment plan only covers future months going forward.
Renter: Prevents the 'cliff' where emergency help ends and you still owe thousands in back arrears.
Provider: Delivers a clean ledger transition after the hardship window with clear forward expectations.
Lawmaker: Contrasts deliberately with France's Visale surety clawback model.
Sources: C-04

II-3Three-source funding braid & mutual dividendsmodeled

Statutory draft
Participation is mandatory statewide upon rollout and funded by three braided legs (~$15.33/unit/month base case at $1,300 rent, ~1.18% of rent): (a) Escrowed deposit net float (~$3.25/unit/mo); (b) Renter-side share (~$6/unit/mo) paid as a monthly advance from the Minnesota Renter's Credit (M1PR) reconciled on the annual return via CRP e-file rails (federal APTC / Form 8962 model; over-cap renters pay ~$6/mo directly, disclosed); and (c) Landlord matched share (~$6/unit/mo) priced on a community-rated base plus bounded experience band, earning a no-claim dividend in surplus years.
Plain language
Roughly $15.33/month per unit comes from three small shares: ~$3.25 from deposit interest float, ~$6 advanced from the existing MN Renter's Credit (or paid directly by high-income renters), and ~$6 matched by the landlord (who gets a rebate dividend in clean years).
Renter: For M1PR-eligible renters (up to $77,570 income), your ~$6/mo share comes from your existing credit — $0 new out-of-pocket.
Provider: ~$6/mo (~$72/yr) replaces $5,600–$7,400 eviction losses, with mutual rebates for clean buildings.
Lawmaker: Rides existing DOR M1PR ($2,720 max) + mandatory CRP e-file rails; split ratio awaits actuarial sign-off.
Sources: C-07, C-08, C-09, C-10, C-11

II-4Year-one reserve ramp & trust-fund backstopopen

Statutory draft
Title II claim payouts phase in as custodial escrow crosses statutory reserve thresholds over the 12-month lease-migration ramp, or via a repayable state startup advance amortized against future premiums on the unemployment-insurance trust-fund precedent.
Plain language
Because deposits move into escrow gradually as leases renew over year one, reserve build is bridged either by a phased coverage start or a repayable state startup loan paid back by future pool premiums.
Renter: Ensures the pool cannot run dry in its first year.
Provider: Guarantees claim solvency from day one of active coverage.
Lawmaker: OPEN DRAFTING CALL: choose reserve-threshold phase-in vs. repayable UI-style startup advance.
Sources: C-09, C-12

II-5Tenant-side actuarial & anti-abuse controlslocked

Statutory draft
Solvency and fairness are enforced on the tenant side through UI-verified hardship triggers, per-lease coverage caps, claims-history pricing tiers at lease renewal, and mandatory cooperation with forward-rent stabilization plans.
Plain language
Protects the pool against gaming: hardship must be documented like unemployment insurance, each lease has a clear cap, repeat claims affect renewal tiering, and forward rent plans require cooperation.
Renter: Keeps premiums tiny (~1.18% of rent) by stopping repeat abuse before it drains the mutual pool.
Provider: Ensures Rent Assurance is a verified hardship bridge, not an open-ended rent holiday.
Lawmaker: Enacts the Fairness Axiom: symmetric guardrails on both sides of the lease.
Sources: C-07, C-08

II-6MHFA subsidized-housing pilot authoritymodeled

Statutory draft
Minnesota Housing (MHFA) is authorized to operate Title II first as a bounded pilot across Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) and agency-financed properties; audited pilot loss ratios and administrative data constitute the empirical fiscal note for statewide rollout.
Plain language
Minnesota Housing can test Rent Assurance first on affordable housing properties it already oversees, producing real Minnesota loss numbers before expanding statewide.
Renter: Starts protection fastest in affordable properties where eviction risk is sharpest.
Provider: Proves claim turnarounds and loss math on real Minnesota buildings before statewide mandate.
Lawmaker: De-risks the legislative fiscal note by generating real in-state actuarial data first.
Sources: C-07

TITLE III — Renter's Belongings Coverage

Integrated contents line (fire, water, theft) riding the same pool; final cap and opt-in mechanics deferred to actuarial review.

III-1Renter's personal belongings line (deferred parameters)open

Statutory draft
Personal-contents coverage for fire, water, and theft rides the same pool infrastructure as a third coverage line, coordinating habitability displacement with Title II as a single integrated claim. Mandatory vs. opt-in election, coverage cap, and deductible remain OPEN pending actuarial pricing.
Plain language
Adds basic protection for a renter's furniture, clothes, and essentials in a fire, flood, or theft — handled in one claim if a building emergency forces a move. Final price/opt-in waits for actuarial math.
Renter: One claim covers both emergency housing stability and replacing essentials after a fire or flood.
Provider: Reduces uninsured tenant displacement friction after building water or fire losses.
Lawmaker: OPEN: ~$10–$15/mo modeled contents load makes opt-in vs. mandatory the main actuarial swing variable.
Sources: C-07

TITLE IV — Governance, Home Agency & Review

Anti-capture board composition, MHFA home agency, administrative appeal standard, and mandatory 5-year sunset.

IV-1Anti-capture governing boardlocked

Statutory draft
The Pool Governing Board includes statutory voting seats reserved for residential tenants and independent consumer advocates alongside housing-provider, actuarial, and agency representatives.
Plain language
Renters and consumer advocates hold guaranteed voting seats on the board that oversees the pool, alongside property owners and insurance experts.
Renter: Renters sit at the table setting rules and reviewing dividends — never locked out.
Provider: Balanced board representation with housing providers and actuaries.
Lawmaker: Structural safeguard against agency or industry capture.

IV-2Home agency & contracted carrier administrationopen

Statutory draft
The Fund is housed at Minnesota Housing (MHFA), claims adjudication sits in an independent administrative body, and day-to-day pool servicing is contracted to a licensed insurer (State Fund Mutual workers'-comp precedent).
Plain language
Minnesota Housing oversees the public fund, an experienced licensed insurer runs day-to-day claims operations, and a separate neutral body decides disputes.
Renter: Professional insurance servicing speed backed by public oversight.
Provider: Claims processed on commercial insurance rails rather than slow agency paperwork.
Lawmaker: OPEN: final confirmation of MHFA vs. Commerce permanent home post-pilot (SFM precedent).

IV-3Administrative review & appeal standardopen

Statutory draft
Adjudication determinations are subject to expedited administrative reconsideration followed by judicial review in Minnesota district court; the statutory standard of review (substantial-evidence record review vs. de novo) is flagged OPEN for legislative counsel.
Plain language
Either side can appeal a referee's decision — first through fast internal review, and then to district court if needed.
Renter: Preserves your constitutional right to court review if an adjudicator errs.
Provider: Clear appeal path for high-value or contested property damage determinations.
Lawmaker: OPEN: select Chapter 14 substantial-evidence review vs. de novo district court standard.

IV-4Five-year sunset & mandatory Year-3 reportlocked

Statutory draft
The Act carries a five-year statutory sunset from statewide effective date, paired with a mandatory Year-3 actuarial, eviction-diversion, and claims-registry audit delivered to the House and Senate housing committees.
Plain language
The law automatically expires after 5 years unless the Legislature votes to renew it after reviewing a full Year-3 audit of real costs, claims, and eviction reductions.
Renter: Public Year-3 report proves whether renters are getting deposits back faster and keeping homes.
Provider: Guarantees the Legislature must fix or end the program if loss ratios miss targets.
Lawmaker: Checkable legislative promise — lowers enactment risk by requiring affirmative renewal.

IV-5Single-subject conforming clauselocked

Statutory draft
All M1PR advance-reconciliation and escrow-float provisions are enacted strictly as funding machinery dedicated to residential rental risk inside this Act, conforming to Minn. Const. art. IV, § 17.
Plain language
Keeps every piece of the bill focused on one constitutional subject — residential rental risk — so the credit-advance mechanics cannot be challenged as a separate tax bill.
Renter: Protects the law against technical constitutional challenges after passage.
Provider: Ensures deposit and assurance funds cannot be raided for unrelated state budgets.
Lawmaker: Drafted explicitly for Minnesota's strict Art. IV, § 17 single-subject jurisprudence.
Sources: C-11
