Where others tried

The record of private rent insurance, deposit-alternative laws, and the public models that actually work — every claim linked to a source you can read yourself.

The pattern across every private attempt: the product called “rental insurance” was never insurance — it is a surety bond. The renter pays nonrefundable fees, stays fully liable for every claim, and gets nothing back at move-out. The public models that work do the opposite: pooled funds, refunds, dispute processes, no monthly fee extraction.

NYC, 2015–2026		 Closed. Laid off 40% of staff in April 2020 after reinsurer Munich Re paused all new policies — COVID unemployment surged and the claims model broke. Raised $121M, merged into Rhino February 2025, then closed entirely in April 2026. layoffs · merger · closure
LeaseLock
California, 2016–		 Forced refunds. First state enforcement action: Maryland’s Attorney General settled with LeaseLock in January 2022, requiring it to refund all monthly payments to Maryland tenants because its deposit waiver violated the state’s refundable-deposit law — plus an injunction against offering the product in Maryland. Law360 · AG release
Obligo
NYC, 2018–		 Same pattern. BBB complaints describe charges for apartments never rented, disputed claims paid while formal disputes are still open, and bank overdrafts from direct withdrawals after landlord claims. BBB complaints
Germany — Mietkautions­versicherung
est. market		 Consumers warned off. Even Europe’s long-established private deposit insurance gets the same verdict from Stiftung Warentest, Germany’s consumer testing authority: ~3–5% of the deposit per year in fees, never refunded, and the insurer recovers any payout from the tenant. Their advice: don’t. Stiftung Warentest · cost breakdown

Public models that work — the contrast

ModelResultVerify
France — Visale
2016–		 Proven at scale. A free public rental guarantee: ~2 million contracts in 10 years, 95% landlord satisfaction, and roughly 80% of covered leases would not have been signed without it. The state pays the landlord immediately, then recovers from the tenant — no monthly fees to the renter. Action Logement · franceinfo · Assemblée nationale
UK — Deposit Protection
2007–		 Proven at scale. Mandatory custodial and insured schemes: 4.7 million deposits protected, only ~1% ever disputed, 90% of tenants get all or part back, and disputes resolve in ~15–21 days. TDS briefing · mydeposits data
Norway — Depositumskonto
statutory		 Closest precedent. By law the deposit sits in a locked account in the tenant’s own name — the landlord pays the account fee, interest belongs to the tenant, and neither party can withdraw alone. Private guarantee products exist alongside — with the same nonrefundable-fee criticism. DNB · dispute board · NTNU housing guide
NCLC policy record
2026		 The verdict is in. The National Consumer Law Center’s May 2026 report Tenant Insecurity documents the harms across all products, and its August 2026 issue brief tells states exactly how to curb them — keep real deposits available and regulate the alternatives. report · issue brief · NHLP guidance (PDF)

What the record says. Private “insurance” extracted fees, kept tenants fully liable, and ended in lawsuits, forced refunds, layoffs, or closure. Public pools — France’s guarantee, the UK’s protected deposits, Norway’s tenant-owned accounts — have protected millions of tenancies for years.

This bill is not an experiment. It is the model that already works where the private version failed — adapted to Minnesota statute.
